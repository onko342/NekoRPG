  • Basic Background:
    • The [Core Reactor] is a piece of [technology] discovered in the Outer-Space Spaceship by Neko.
    • It has good energy reflection abilities, and can coax out the energy in [A7·Energy Cores] and [B1·Energy Cores].
    • This energy can be used to make super high-strength [Gel Sword Hilts], or condensed into [Basic Evolution Crystals].

  • B1·Energy Core - Breeder Fuel:
    • Increases [Radiation] by the formula 0.4log(n+1)x.
    • Enters a supercritical state when there are [317] in the reactor, causing [Radiation] to grow exponentially.
    • Heat value is [8000], meaning that 1 B1·Energy Core can release 8000 [Energy] when fully depleted.

  • A7·Energy Core - Regular Fuel:
    • Increases reactor [Radiation] by the formula 0.4sqrt(nx).
    • Will never enter a [Supercritical] state. Disregarding other sources of [Radiation], 2% will be used per second.
    • Has a heat value of [20]. Means that 1 A7·Energy Core can release 20 [Energy] when fully depleted.

  • Lightning Blessing - Radiation Source:
    • Releases [Radiation] by the formula 0.001n.
    • Compared to the previous two types of fuel, its greatest advantage is that it doesn't need any [Radiation] to activate.
    • Heat value [1]. When a Lightning Blessing is fully depleted, it will only have released 1 [Energy].
    • Can be used to [fully deplete] the previous two types of fuel, but it will never be [fully depleted] itself.

  • High-Energy Gel - Coolant:
    • Increases the heat capacity of the reactor pile by the formula 0.01n (Ratio between [Radiation] and [Change in temperature])
    • Can also cool the reactor when added in the middle of the reaction. However, some of the energy will be diluted and wasted.
    • The energy dilution mechanic has been removed. Now you can add High-Energy Gel without worry./li>
    • Increasing heat capacity is not without cost. By the square-cube rule, the rate of natural cooling is n^-1/3, decreasing when adding more gel.
    • This means that the reactor cools by -10%/s when there are 10 units of [High-Energy Gel], a figure that diminishes to -1%/s when where are 10000.

  • Piston - Compress / Expand:
    • [Arctic Air] is a type of single atom, and is a gas that is extremely close to an ideal gas.
    • As a result, its thermal insulation coefficient is roughly 5/3, meaning that in thermal insulation actions, the rate of change of pressure is 5/3 times the rate of change of volume.
    • Anyone who has learned chemistry should know, that the other 2/3 becomes heat energy!
    • When the piston is in the outside environment, temperature approaches the ambient temperature with a speed of 0.1(T2-T1).
    • The default ambient temperature of the Arctic Tundra is 240K.
    • This represents heat conduction.The latter represents heat radiation. It takes a dominant position when above 1200K.
    • Basically, after you let the excess heat be released, you can release your hand and let the gas [expand].
    • Then, its temperature will decrease, cooling the insulated bag.
    • Additionally, the inflation limit is 30m³ and the compression limit is 0.3m³.
    • Neko is incompetent, the bottom of the piston is uneven, can't press it any further...
    • Why was heat radiation deleted?
    • Originally there was a heat radiation mechanic here. But unfortunately, it is extremely unstable above 12000K, the simulation breaks down and keeps producing NaN values.
    • This means that in just 1 frame, it would radiate its heat to a huge negative temperature.
    • Although I could use a differential equation to check the true rate of change, that is too much work.
    • So, before Sayuki thinks of a better method... I guess we don't really need it.

  • Piston - Pump Air / Release Air:
    • You want to increase production? No problem!
    • Initially, there is 100 million mol of Arctic Air inside the piston, but obviously you can stuff more inside.
    • More air means harder compression, but the heat capacity of the air will also increase.
    • If your insulated bag has a lot of Arctic Superfluid, it is pretty much necessary to add more Arctic Air.
    • The process even releases a bit of heat, but because cold air will enter to mix, the temperature won't increase by much.
    • Additionally, the default pressure of the Arctic Tundra is 12MPa/120bar/118.4308atm.
    • As a result, releasing air will only decrease pressure to a limit of 12MPa.
    • Right, if anyone is interested, the approximation made in air pumping calculations is:
    • It waits for the pumped air to be compressed to the inner pressure under insulated conditions, then compresses the entire thing while insulated, then lastly waits for heat equilibrium.
    • Result:
    • $$ \frac{dN}{dt} = \frac{P}{RT_1[\frac{25}{6}(\frac{p_2}{p_1})^{\frac{2}{5}}-\frac{5}{2}]} $$
    • $$ dT = \frac{dN}{N}[\frac{5T_1}{3}(\frac{p_2}{p_1})^{\frac{2}{5}}-T_2] $$
    • dN/dt is the rate of air accumulation (mol/s), N is the total amount of air (mol), P is power, R is the ideal gas constant, T2 is the inner temperature, p2 is the inner pressure, T1 is the outer temperature, p1 is the outer pressure, and dT is the rate of change of temperature.
    • Remember: Don't bite off more than you can chew...
    • If you really added too much air, compression will become incredibly difficult, and take a lot of time.
    • Thankfully, there is a [Release Air] button: every second the amount of air inside the piston will become 1/e times the original amount. Be careful to not release too much!
    • If you need to do this, you obviously bit off more than you can chew.

  • Arctic Superfluid / Heat Exchange:
    • It's a material with extremely high heat conductivity, with relaxation time less than 1 frame [40ms]
    • To reduce the amount of heat absorbed from the outside as much as possible, it's always piled into a sphere.
    • When the piston is inside the insulated bag, it will undergo heat exchange with the Arctic Superfluid.
    • 1m³ Arctic Superfluid has heat capacity equal to 10'000'000 mol Arctic Air.
    • The exchanged heat is proportional to converted heat capacity: Q1Q2/(Q1+Q2) and temperature difference.
    • Simply put, if one side has very small heat capacity, converted heat capacity approaches it.
    • So try to keep your Arctic Superfluid and Arctic Air heat capacity the same1
    • Additionally, newly added Arctic Superfluid has the same temperature as the outside.
    • This means that adding more to heavily cooled Arctic Superfluid will dilute the heat difference even more.

  • Porous Ice Crystal / Insulation Layer:
    • Taking into account that heat exchange rate is inversely proportional to thickness, when extending the Cauchy inequality to infinite area infinitesimal elements, we find that:
    • Uniformly distributing the insulation layer on the surface of the Arctic Superfluid is the best choice for insulating.
    • The thickness of the insulating layer uses the difference between two sphere radii, 1 pts Porous Ice Crystal = 0.1m³
    • Therefore, increasing the amount of Arctic Superfluid not only increases surface area, and also decreases insulation layer thickness.
    • Instantaneous temperature change is equal to 0.001*area*temperature difference between insulated bag and environment/volume/insulation layer thickness.
    • Under initial conditions, this means 4.6%/s, or a heat gap half-life of 14.9s.
    • There is no downside to adding more insulation! Unless you compressed gas inside the insulated bag...

  • Ice Element / Myriad Year Ice Marrow:
    • Arctic Superfluid turns into [Myriad Year Ice Marrow] when more than 1000pts/m³ of [Ice Element] is accumulated.
    • [Ice Element] is a sprite of subatomic scale. These sprites tell molecules to not move when they want to move.
    • I don't know why, but the rate of [Ice Element] accumulation is the exact opposite of normal chemical reaction rates.
    • Compared to the usual v=Ae^(-Ep/RT), [Ice Element] unit area accumulation rate is σ=a*e^(b/T).
    • When the process finishes in 100s at 80K and takes 10000 years at 240K,
    • we get a=3.4764e-14, b=2623.17.
    • What I mean is that, decreasing the temperature by a bit can greatly increase the rate of ice element accumulation!
    • So, try to decrease the temperature inside the insulated bag! It's worth it to decrease the amount of Arctic Superfluid!
    • Interesting fact: this means that the activation energy of the ice element capture reaction is -21.80kJ/mol.
    • Because it can't flow after becoming [Myriad Year Ice Marrow], the [Ice Element] accumulation cap is also 1000pts/m³.
    • To prevent floating point error, you can take out [Myriad Year Ice Marrow] when the [Ice Element] concentration exceeds 999.999 pts/m³.
